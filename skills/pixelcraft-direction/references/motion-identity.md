# Motion identity

Motion identity is the consistent character of movement across a brand/product, not a library or a bag of effects.

## Define a motion language

Specify:
- **tempo:** immediate, measured, energetic, ceremonial, or deliberately slow;
- **easing character:** precise, elastic, weighted, abrupt, fluid, or restrained;
- **spatial behavior:** planar, depth-aware, path-following, or static;
- **stagger grammar:** which items sequence and what order communicates;
- **feedback style:** how hover, focus, press, loading, success, and error acknowledge input;
- **signature moment:** one memorable transition or movement with a product/brand reason.

## Consistency rules

- Reuse a small motion token vocabulary rather than scattering durations/eases.
- Keep operational feedback faster and clearer than marketing choreography.
- Use the signature moment sparingly; do not turn every heading, card, and button into a spectacle.
- Keep movement consistent with material/voice but never let identity override accessibility or task success.
- Name the reduced-motion equivalent before implementation.

## Reference analysis

When learning from creative sites, record motion grammar (timing, direction, sequence, interruption, input), not only effect labels. A GSAP tag does not define the visual identity.
